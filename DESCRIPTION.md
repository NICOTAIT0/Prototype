Huddle Check helps a team leader see in minutes whether a meeting is wasting the team's expertise. The leader pastes a meeting transcript, the team charter, and Breakout Learning or assessment feedback. The tool returns an instant participation read plus AI coaching and a plan for the next meeting. Built-in instructions and sample data let other leaders use their own.

The design draws on each week's readings. From Week 1 (Intro to Teams), actual performance equals potential minus process losses, and planning prevents them. The charter check therefore tests for a clear purpose, a measurable goal, defined roles, and someone who integrates specialized work. Following Larson and LaFasto, structure should match the task, so leaders select a meeting purpose (creative, decision, problem, or tactical), which changes the advice.

From Week 2 (Building and Managing), participation follows an 80/20 pattern, and confidence is not the same as expertise (Kruger and Dunning). The tool compares each person's share of words to an equal share and names spectators whose expertise the team is not using.

From Week 3 (Conflict and Creativity), teams should discuss ideas before voting, and task conflict helps while relationship conflict does not (Eisenhardt, Kolb, Shah et al.). The tool flags concerns that were answered by moving on and recommends a parking lot and ground rules.

From Week 4 (Influence and Decision-Making), the tool flags when one person closes most decisions and suggests round-robins, direct questions, and rotating facilitators. Because transcripts cannot show psychological safety, the feedback input fills that gap.

The instant check uses simple pattern matching, so results are prompts for discussion, not verdicts.
